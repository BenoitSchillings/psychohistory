Seldon in two minutes

Version 58, 11 November 2026. The short version of the full report; how it works and how it is tested is in the methodology.

Seldon simulates the next forty years for 175 countries, ten thousand times, from eighty years of history. Read it less as an oracle than as an instrument: near the present it makes forecasts that are scored as reality arrives; far out it shows which beliefs about AI, climate and nuclear escalation actually move the outcome.

Track record. Trained on data to 2024, it ranked 2025's one new civil war (South Sudan) 3rd of 142 countries. Its monthly fatality layer ranks 2nd of 21 in the 2023/24 ViEWS challenge. In hindcasts its country forecasts beat fair naive forecasts at every horizon up to 15 years (+11% on who is at war, +22% on new wars at 15 years). Forecasting crowds beat it on near-term questions about wars between states (Brier 0.100 against 0.130); where a crowd covers a question, trust the crowd. Every forecast is frozen and scored as UCDP publishes.

Explorations to 2065: what follows from stated assumptions

The long run hinges on when AI makes intellectual work nearly free. The worlds split by that, so the pooled median ($36k) describes few actual worlds. World output per person in 2065 (median, 90% band):

A war between great powers by 2065 happens in 43% of the headline worlds, but read it as somewhere between 18% and 76%: that is the spread across draws of the assumptions. The number has also moved between model versions, from 69% in October to 43% today, through bug fixes and design choices; model choices are part of the uncertainty. Counting only the traditional great powers, 21%. Nuclear weapons are used in 12% of worlds (2%–26%).

Known biases, and which way they push

  • Wars between states: too low. It missed the 2010s rise two- to threefold, and it gave the 2025 Thailand–Cambodia war under 1% and the 2026 US–Iran war 0.1%. Its rivalry data end in 2010, so recent rivalries are partly invisible.
  • The safest countries: too high. It expected two or three new civil wars among the 20 safest countries in 15-year windows since 1995; none happened.
  • World recessions: too rare. Simulated world output falls in under 2% of years, against 3–10% recorded.
  • Single countries' income: bands too wide. Countries drift apart 1.3–1.9 times too much since the 1990s.
  • World growth: no stable direction. In 15-year hindcasts it ran 11–18% high from 1970s starts and 3–17% low from 1990s starts, so the headline is not corrected.
