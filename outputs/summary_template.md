**Explorations to 2065: what follows from stated assumptions, not forecasts**

These are shares of ten thousand simulated worlds. They depend on assumptions history cannot test, above all when AI arrives and how readily nuclear-armed rivals escalate, and on a war model fitted to 1946–2024. Each comes with what moves it.

- **Humanity gets richer, but how much depends on AI.** World output per person grows from {gdp_now} in 2025 to a median {gdp_med} in 2065, with a 90% range from {gdp_lo} to {gdp_hi}. Without human-level AI the median is {gdp_noai}. (The arrival dates mix three surveys' definitions of human-level AI; the economics assumes a modest, energy-capped growth dividend once it arrives, not near-free cognition.) When AI and robots arrive explains {ai_share} of that range, more than any other assumption. [The fan of futures →](#s-world)
- **The gains go to the frontier.** Countries that build and adopt AI first, and the firms that own it, capture most of the gain; middle-income exporters lose ground as robots bring factory work home. Who keeps the rents moves inequality and polarization more than world output. [AI and the race →](#s-ai)
- **Other breakthroughs change economies, not politics.** Seventeen possible breakthroughs, from energy storage and fission to engineered crops and longevity drugs, are drawn in every world. Together they add {tech_gdp} to world output, cut oil prices by {tech_oil} and grain by {tech_grain}, and lift the Sahel by {tech_sahel}, but leave civil war, coups and democracy where they were. [Technology breakthroughs →](#card-tech)
- **Warming has a real cost, largest where it is already hot.** On the middle emissions path, world output per person is {clim_245_2065} lower in 2065 and {clim_245_2100} lower in 2100 than in the same worlds without warming damage (up to {clim_245_2100_hi} lower in the worst 5%); on the fossil-fuelled path, {clim_585_2100} lower by 2100. It also kills: on the middle path about {heat_245_2065} more people die of heat by 2065 than milder winters save, {heat_245_2100} by 2100 ({heat_585_2100} on the fossil-fuelled path). The cost falls hardest on the Sahel and the tropics. Where breakthroughs arrive (clean energy beyond the path, carbon removal, sometimes geoengineering), the middle path's 2100 cost falls to about {clim_tech_2100}. [Forty years of climate →](#s-climate)
- **Civil war stays near its post-1946 high.** {conf_now} of countries were at war in 2025; the median path is {conf_2065} in 2065, and in {p_record} of worlds the 1990 record is broken at least once. [Where the risk concentrates →](#s-risk)
- **A great-power war is a real tail risk, and an assumption moves it a lot.** {gpw_cow} of the simulated worlds see a war between the traditional great powers by 2065. Counting India as a great power (nuclear-armed, over 5% of world output) raises it to {gpw}, mostly through a China–India war; between nuclear-armed states, crises have so far stopped short of war. Read as a credence, it ranges from {gpw_cred_lo} to {gpw_cred_hi} across draws of every assumption (5th to 95th percentile of blocks of worlds sharing one draw); the assumed escalation between nuclear-armed rivals alone moves it from {gpw_nuc_lo} to {gpw_nuc_hi}. For comparison, the post-1945 record gives 24–63% for 40 years, depending on whether one counts from 1946 or after the Korean War, and whether India counts. A nuclear weapon is used in {nuke} of worlds, almost always once; a large exchange in {nuke_large}. [Wars between states →](#s-inter)
- **Democracy barely recovers.** The world's population-weighted democracy index, {dem_now} in 2025 after two decades of decline, is about {dem_2065} in 2065 (90% range {dem_lo} to {dem_hi}): personalist rule and pressure on free expression hold back the recovery that followed earlier waves of decline. The United States has a {us_aut} chance of another large fall by 2030. [Every finding →](#s-narr)
- **The worst futures have traceable causes.** In the worst 1% of worlds, output collapses when AI comes late, big economies stall and the heaviest climate damage holds; mass death needs a great-power war, often with nuclear use. An AI loss-of-control catastrophe killing 1–10% of the world's population strikes {ai_cat} of worlds by 2065. Its rate is an assumption anchored to forecasters' and experts' estimates for a larger event, one killing a tenth of humanity, so it is high for an event of this size. [The worst 1% →](#s-disaster)
