### Root causes, in plain words

- **Economic collapse is a slow slide, and now partly a climate slide.** In the worst 1% of worlds, world GDP per person in 2065 is about $12k, a third below today's. About 78% of the gap to a middle world is weaker underlying growth in the big economies (China, the United States and India account for most of it) and 21% is climate damage; war damage adds under 1%. These worlds mostly drew late or no near-free AI and the largest climate damage estimate (global temperature, Bilal & Känzig 2024): switching climate damage off removes 90% of the tail, and giving every world the smallest damage estimate removes 88%. Without AI the tail is 2.6 times larger. Historically, world GDP per person has not fallen over any 40-year span in two centuries: this tail is the model's extrapolation of ageing workforces, no AI, a run of bad decades and the high end of climate damage, not a pattern seen before.
- **Mass war deaths need a great-power war.** Every world in the worst 1% (median about 40 million war and nuclear deaths) has one, and a third see nuclear use. Allies joining wars matter most: without alliance cascades the death tail halves. Without near-free AI it shrinks by about 45% (AI races raise war risk in the model, an assumption).
- **The age of civil war starts with a wave and is carried by shared conditions.** In the worst 1%, over 40% of countries are at war on average. Nearly every one begins with a wave of new civil wars, and no single shock (pandemic, oil, great-power war) is more common in these worlds than elsewhere. Remove the shared world mood and the tail shrinks by 41%; remove the climate tails and channels (mostly the assumed link from warming to civil war in farming countries) and it shrinks by 16%; remove lasting country war-proneness and it shrinks by 12%. Known weakness: the model's structural-uncertainty term pushes very-low-risk countries (Nordic and Baltic states, Luxembourg) into war together in these worlds; its redesign is the top open item.
- **The authoritarian age is an AI assumption.** It comes with early AI and the assumed drift toward autocracy during the transition; without AI the tail shrinks by about 80%. These are also among the richest worlds.

### What the search found wrong in the model
- **Too much volatility in the largest economies.** Each country's yearly growth surprise was drawn from a pool that ignored size, so China and India swung like small countries. Scaling by population (large countries are calmer, 1960–2024) improved ten-year world GDP hindcasts by 7% (1970–1998) and 2% (2000–2012) and was adopted. World growth still falls in about one simulated year in five against one in thirty historically: pandemics, AI bubbles and wars add yearly swings that history did not have at this frequency, and this is not yet checked against data.
- **The oil price wanders too far.** By 2065 the 5–95% range runs from about 0.13 to 7 times the 2025 price; real oil has stayed within roughly 0.15 to 1.6 times today's level since 1861. Its pull toward the long-run average is weak (5% a year), and falling demand from clean energy is applied as a permanent price drift. Not yet fixed.
