- **Humanity gets richer, but how much depends on AI.** World output per person grows from $18k in 2025 to a median $36k in 2065, with a 90% range from $18k to $92k. Without near-free cognition the median is $25k. When AI arrives explains about a third of that range, more than any other assumption. [The fan of futures →](#s-world)
- **The gains go to the frontier.** Countries that build and adopt AI first, and the firms that own it, capture most of the gain; middle-income exporters lose ground as robots bring factory work home. Who keeps the rents moves inequality and polarization more than world output. [AI and the race →](#s-ai)
- **Other breakthroughs change economies, not politics.** Seventeen possible breakthroughs, from storage and fission to engineered crops and longevity drugs, are drawn in every world. Together they add about 2.5% to world output, cut oil prices by a sixth and grain by a tenth, and lift the Sahel by about 8%, but leave civil war, coups and democracy where they were. [Technology breakthroughs →](#card-tech)
- **Warming has a real cost, largest where it is already hot.** On the middle emissions path, world output per person is 14% lower in 2065 and 27% lower in 2100 than in the same worlds without warming damage (90% range: no loss to 52% lower by 2100); on the fossil-fuelled path, 43% lower by 2100. The cost falls hardest on the Sahel and the tropics. Where breakthroughs arrive (clean energy beyond the path, carbon removal, sometimes geoengineering), the middle path's 2100 cost falls to about 10%. [Forty years of climate →](#s-climate)
- **Civil war stays near its post-1946 high.** About one country in five was at war in 2025; the median path stays near 22% through 2065, and in two worlds in three the 1990 record is broken at least once. The next five years' likeliest new wars are in Chad, Libya and Uganda; coups cluster in Niger, Guinea and Madagascar. [Where the risk concentrates →](#s-risk)
- **A great-power war is a real tail risk.** Nearly half of the simulated worlds see a war between great powers by 2065, counting India; a quarter among the traditional majors alone. A nuclear weapon is used in about one world in eight, almost always once; a large exchange happens in fewer than one in two hundred. War size, not war count, decides the death toll. [Wars between states →](#s-inter)
- **Democracy barely recovers.** The world's population-weighted democracy index, 0.38 in 2025 after two decades of decline, is about 0.40 in 2065 (90% range 0.29 to 0.53): personalist rule and pressure on free expression hold back the recovery that followed earlier waves of decline. The United States has about a one-in-four chance of another large fall by 2030. [Every finding →](#s-narr)
- **The worst futures have traceable causes.** In the worst 1% of worlds, output collapses when AI comes late, big economies stall and the heaviest climate damage holds; mass death needs a great-power war, often with nuclear use. [The worst 1% →](#s-disaster)
- **How far to trust it.** Trained only on data to 2024, the model ranked 2025's one new civil war 3rd of 142 countries. Its monthly layer ranks 2nd of 21 in the 2023/24 VIEWS forecasting challenge. Where prediction markets or crowds cover a near-term question, they beat the model, and the report shows their numbers. [Track record →](#part-trust)
