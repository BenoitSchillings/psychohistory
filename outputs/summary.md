**Forecasts: scored against what happens, to 2030**

- **Where fighting could start in the next twelve months.** Among countries with no month of 25 battle deaths in the past year, the likeliest to have one by September 2027 are Benin (39%), Burundi (35%) and Togo (27%). Every monthly forecast is frozen and scored as UCDP publishes. [Forecasts logged before the fact →](#s-ledger)
- **Where crises are likeliest, 2026–2030.** The likeliest new civil wars are in Chad, Libya and Uganda; the likeliest coups in Niger, Guinea and Madagascar. The United States has a 26% chance of another large democratic fall by 2030. Each number is shown with its model range and against the average country's risk; they rank where to look harder, not what to do. [Where the risk concentrates →](#s-risk)
- **How far to trust it.** Trained only on data to 2024, the model ranked 2025's one new civil war 3rd of 142 countries. Its monthly layer ranks 2nd of 21 in the 2023/24 VIEWS forecasting challenge. Where prediction markets or crowds cover a near-term question, they beat the model, and the report shows their numbers. [Track record →](#part-trust)

**Explorations to 2065: what follows from stated assumptions, not forecasts**

These are shares of ten thousand simulated worlds. They depend on assumptions history cannot test, above all when AI arrives and how readily nuclear-armed rivals escalate, and on a war model fitted to 1946–2024. Each comes with what moves it.

- **Humanity gets richer, but how much depends on AI.** World output per person grows from $18k in 2025 to a median $34k in 2065, with a 90% range from $18k to $88k. Without near-free cognition the median is $24k. When AI and robots arrive explains 38% of that range, more than any other assumption. [The fan of futures →](#s-world)
- **The gains go to the frontier.** Countries that build and adopt AI first, and the firms that own it, capture most of the gain; middle-income exporters lose ground as robots bring factory work home. Who keeps the rents moves inequality and polarization more than world output. [AI and the race →](#s-ai)
- **Other breakthroughs change economies, not politics.** Seventeen possible breakthroughs, from energy storage and fission to engineered crops and longevity drugs, are drawn in every world. Together they add 2.5% to world output, cut oil prices by 16% and grain by 10%, and lift the Sahel by 9%, but leave civil war, coups and democracy where they were. [Technology breakthroughs →](#card-tech)
- **Warming has a real cost, largest where it is already hot.** On the middle emissions path, world output per person is 14% lower in 2065 and 27% lower in 2100 than in the same worlds without warming damage (up to 52% lower in the worst 5%); on the fossil-fuelled path, 43% lower by 2100. It also kills: on the middle path about 14 million more people die of heat by 2065 than milder winters save, 62 million by 2100 (214 million on the fossil-fuelled path). The cost falls hardest on the Sahel and the tropics. Where breakthroughs arrive (clean energy beyond the path, carbon removal, sometimes geoengineering), the middle path's 2100 cost falls to about 10%. [Forty years of climate →](#s-climate)
- **Civil war stays near its post-1946 high.** 18% of countries were at war in 2025; the median path is 23% in 2065, and in 78% of worlds the 1990 record is broken at least once. [Where the risk concentrates →](#s-risk)
- **A great-power war is a real tail risk, and an assumption moves it a lot.** 45% of the simulated worlds see a war between great powers by 2065, counting India; 21% among the traditional majors alone. The assumed escalation between nuclear-armed rivals alone moves the first from 31% to 55%. For comparison, the post-1945 record gives 24–63% for 40 years, depending on whether one counts from 1946 or after the Korean War, and whether India counts. A nuclear weapon is used in 13% of worlds, almost always once; a large exchange in 0.3%. [Wars between states →](#s-inter)
- **Democracy barely recovers.** The world's population-weighted democracy index, 0.38 in 2025 after two decades of decline, is about 0.40 in 2065 (90% range 0.29 to 0.53): personalist rule and pressure on free expression hold back the recovery that followed earlier waves of decline. The United States has a 26% chance of another large fall by 2030. [Every finding →](#s-narr)
- **The worst futures have traceable causes.** In the worst 1% of worlds, output collapses when AI comes late, big economies stall and the heaviest climate damage holds; mass death needs a great-power war, often with nuclear use. An AI loss-of-control catastrophe, drawn as an assumption between forecasters' and experts' estimates, strikes 1.9% of worlds by 2065. [The worst 1% →](#s-disaster)
